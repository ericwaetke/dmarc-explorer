Common Reasons for DMARC Failures

  • Third-Party Senders: Services like Mailchimp, Sendgrid, or Google Workspace may be sending emails on your behalf, but their IPs aren't listed in your domain's SPF record, or you haven't set up DKIM keys for them.
  • Email Forwarding: When a recipient forwards an email, the forwarding server's IP will usually fail SPF. To survive forwarding, the email must have a valid DKIM signature.
  • Misconfigured SPF/DKIM: Syntax errors in your SPF TXT record or improperly rotated DKIM keys can cause legitimate emails to fail authentication.
  • Malicious Spoofing: The source IP might belong to a spammer or attacker attempting to spoof your domain. If you don't recognize the IP or service, DMARC is successfully protecting your domain by rejecting it.
